French has a reputation as the language of love, but textbooks rarely teach you
what people _actually say_ when they meet someone they like. Here are 31
expressions, in the order a real encounter tends to go: breaking the ice,
being curious about the other one, showing interest,
planning a date, and — if all goes well — saying the big
words.

Most of these are informal (they use _tu_ and drop the _ne_ of negations, the
way spoken French does). A few use _vous_ on purpose.

## Breaking the ice

Getting-to-know-you questions. Short, casual, and very common in a bar or at a
party.

1. **"tu viens d'où ?"** — _where are you from?_
   The standard way to ask someone where he or she is from. This will be asked to you
   as soon as someone hears you have an accent.
   In French casual communication, we don't invert verb and the subject.
2. **"tu fais quoi dans la vie ?"** — _what do you do for a living?_
   Literally "you do what in the life?". That's the most natural way to ask for someone occupation or job.
   Alternatives would be: "tu travailles dans quoi ?", or "c'est quoi ton boulot"
3. **"t'as quel âge ?"** — _how old are you?_
   _T'as_ is the spoken contraction of _tu as_. You will hear it constantly.
4. **"t'as un copain / une copine ?"** — _do you have a boyfriend / a girlfriend?_
   _Copain / copine_ can also mean "friend" — context (and tone) makes the
   difference.
5. **"t'habites où ?"** — _where do you live?_
   Useful to know if it worth it to go with someone ;)

## Showing interest

6. **"tu me plais"** — _I like you_
   One way to say that you like someone. Literally "you please me".
   This is a question of taste, not on the same level as "je t'aime", no feelings are involved yet
7. **"t'es drôle"** — _you're funny_
   A safe expression to compliment someone's sense of humour without saying he or she's a clown
   You can also say "t'es marrant" or "j'aime ton sens de l'humour"
8. **"t'as de beaux yeux"** — _you have beautiful eyes_
   It's not something you'll hear a lot, because it's a bit too much, too romantic but it's
   sometimes said in a playful tone refering to the 1938 film _Le Quai des brumes_ ("T'as d'beaux yeux, tu
   sais") - a classic. Slightly cliché, and everyone knows it, say it with a smile.
9. **"vous êtes charmant(e)"** — _you are charming_
   With _vous_: more formal, more elegant, more old-school.
   To use when someone is triggering something in you not necessarily due to his/her beauty
10. **"vous êtes élégant(e)"** — _you're elegant_
    When someone is dressed pretty well, use that expression
11. **"tu dis ça à toutes les filles"** — _you say that to all the girls_
    The playful comeback to a compliment. Keeps the conversation light.
    Often used by girls to discourage boys when they seem too confident.

## Getting the number, planning the date

12. **"j'aimerais bien te revoir"** — _I'd like to see you again_
    The polite, clear way to say you are interested in a second meeting.
13. **"donne-moi ton numéro de téléphone"** — _give me your phone number_
    Direct. Soften it with _tu me donnes ton numéro ?_ if that feels too
    blunt. A more authoritan way to say it would be "je vais prendre ton numéro de téléphone" or "passe-moi ton numéro"
14. **"je t'appelle"** — _I'll call you_
    When leaving someone that you plan to meet very soonn, just say "je t'appelle".
    It shows a strong intention to see the other person again
15. **"je t'envoie un message / un texto"** — _I'll send you a message / a text_
    You can replace "message", "Texto". "SMS" is less and less used
16. **"tu veux boire un verre ce soir ?"** — _want to grab a drink tonight?_
    The standard first-date proposal. _Boire un verre_ = "to have a drink".
17. **"je ne suis pas libre ce soir"** — _I'm not free tonight_
    A gentle no — or a "not tonight".
    But please propose other dates if you're interested
18. **"demain, je suis disponible"** — _I'm free tomorrow_
    Putting the time first is a natural way to emphasise it.
19. **"on se voit ce soir"** — _see you tonight_
    _On_ replaces _nous_ in almost all spoken French.
20. **"désolé, je suis en retard"** — _sorry, I'm late_
    You will need this one. Add an _-e_ (_désolée_) if you are a woman.
21. **"je suis content(e) de te revoir"** — _I am happy to see you again_
    The second date opener.

## Going home

22. **"tu veux que je te ramène ?"** — _want me to give you a ride home?_
    _Ramener_ = to bring someone back. Note the subjunctive _que je te ramène_.
23. **"tu peux dormir à la maison / sur le canapé"** — _you can sleep at my place / on the couch_
    _À la maison_ means "at home" — mine, in this case. Adding _sur le canapé_
    makes the intention (more) innocent.
24. **"pas le premier soir"** — _not on the first date_
    Literally "not the first evening". Clear, and perfectly common.
25. **"j'ai envie de t'embrasser"** — _I want to kiss you_
    _Avoir envie de_ = to feel like / to want. Careful: _embrasser_ means "to
    kiss", not "to embrace".

## When it gets serious

26. **"tu me manques"** — _I miss you_
    One of the most famous French inversions: literally "you are missing to
    me". The person missed is the subject.
27. **"je pense à toi tout le temps"** — _I think about you all the time_
28. **"j'ai besoin de toi"** — _I need you_
    _Avoir besoin de_ + stressed pronoun (_toi_, not _tu_).
29. **"ne me quitte pas"** — _don't leave me_
    Also the title of Jacques Brel's most famous song. Dramatic, by design.
30. **"je ne peux pas vivre sans toi"** — _I can't live without you_
31. **"je t'aime"** — _I love you_
    The big one. Be careful with the adverb: _je t'aime bien_ means "I like
    you (as a friend)" — adding _bien_ actually weakens it.
