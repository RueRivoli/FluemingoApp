Your memory does not decay at a constant rate. It drops fast at first, then
slows. Spaced repetition exploits that curve.

## The forgetting curve

Learn a word today and you will have lost most of it within a few days — unless
you meet it again. Each successful review flattens the curve, and the next review
can wait longer.

## What that means in practice

| Review | Typical interval |
| ------ | ---------------- |
| 1st    | 1 day            |
| 2nd    | 3 days           |
| 3rd    | 1 week           |
| 4th    | 3 weeks          |
| 5th    | 2 months         |

The intervals are not magic numbers; they adapt to how easily you recalled the
word. A word you hesitated on comes back sooner.

## Why it feels harder than rereading

Because it is. Retrieval — pulling the word out of your memory — is more
effortful than recognising it on a page, and that effort is precisely what makes
it stick. If your reviews feel comfortable, they are probably too frequent.

## The only rule

Do the reviews when they are due. A spaced repetition system that you check twice
a week is just a vocabulary list.
